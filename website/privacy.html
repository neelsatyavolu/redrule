Privacy policy

Last updated September 23, 2026

Redrule records and transcribes meetings on your Mac. This page explains what stays on your Mac, what leaves it, and where it goes. Redrule has no accounts or ads. It sends one anonymous usage ping a day, which you can turn off.

What stays on your Mac

  • Redrule records the call's audio and your microphone. It transcribes both on your Mac with NVIDIA Parakeet, run by sherpa-onnx. Speaker recognition also runs on your Mac.
  • Audio is not saved to disk unless you turn on Keep audio recordings in Settings.
  • Meetings, transcripts, notes and tags are plain files in ~/Library/Application Support/Redrule/meetings. Deleting a meeting in Redrule deletes its folder.
  • Sign-in tokens and API keys are stored in your Mac's Keychain.

Note writing and questions

You choose in Settings where notes are written and questions are answered.

  • On your Mac. A Qwen3.5 model runs locally. The transcript does not leave your Mac.
  • With a provider you choose. You can sign in to ChatGPT (through Codex) or Grok, or add your own API key for OpenAI, Anthropic, Google Gemini or an OpenAI-compatible service. Redrule then sends the meeting's transcript, and for questions its notes and your question, from your Mac to that provider. There is no Redrule server in between. The provider's own terms and privacy policy apply to what it receives.

Calendar

If you allow calendar access, Redrule reads the title and attendee names of the event that matches a meeting. It reads them on your Mac, to name the meeting and to give context when notes are written. They leave your Mac only as part of a note-writing request to the provider you chose. With the local model, they stay on your Mac.

Sharing

Nothing is shared unless you choose Share or add a meeting to a shared folder.

  • Sharing uploads the note, and the transcript and speaker names if you include them, to Redrule's sharing service. The service runs on Vercel and stores shared meetings in Vercel Blob.
  • A shared folder also shows the meeting's title and time, and the name you chose to appear as its recorder.
  • Anyone with the link can read what you shared. Links are long and random, and shared pages ask search engines not to index them.
  • Stop sharing deletes the hosted copy. So does removing a meeting from a shared folder or deleting the folder. Copies that others already saved cannot be recalled.
  • Audio is never uploaded.
  • To limit abuse, the service may process IP addresses, for example to rate-limit requests. Vercel also keeps standard request logs.

Crash reports

Crash reports are off by default. If you turn them on in Settings, Redrule sends a report to Sentry when it crashes. A report contains the error message, the stack trace, the app version and your Mac's model and macOS version. Your home folder name and anything that looks like a key or token are removed before it is sent. Recordings, transcripts and notes are never included, though an error message can name a file.

Usage stats

Once a day, Redrule sends an anonymous ping to analytics.n3el.dev, a service the developer runs to count how many copies are in use. The ping contains a random install ID made on your Mac, the app version, the macOS version and whether the Mac has an Apple or Intel chip. The service notes the country the request came from but does not store your IP address. The ping never includes meeting content, names, emails, file paths or account details. To stop it, turn off Share anonymous usage stats in Settings.

Updates and downloads

  • A minute after launch, and then every six hours, Redrule checks GitHub for a newer version and downloads updates from there.
  • The speech models download from GitHub. The local notes models download from Hugging Face.
  • The list of available ChatGPT and Grok models is fetched from GitHub.
  • Once a day, unless you turn it off, the usage ping described above.

These requests contain no meeting content. Like any web request, they reveal your IP address to the host.

This website

This website runs on Vercel, which keeps standard request logs. Pages load fonts from Google Fonts, and the home page asks GitHub for the latest version number. The home, privacy and terms pages count visits with analytics.n3el.dev: the page address, the referring site, your country, a rough browser and device type, and clicks on the download link. It sets no cookies and stores no IP address, and it can't follow you from one day to the next. Shared meeting pages are not counted.
